The Bureau

the bureau of observability was established in order to observe. what it observes, principally, is itself. the metrics are excellent. nothing else is.

§ 01

A Brief History

abridged, as required

The Bureau was founded to watch over the network. In time, it emerged that the network required watching over by a second bureau, which was founded, and that this second bureau required oversight, for which a third was founded. The third bureau’s charter was lost in 1987, and with it the second bureau’s reason to exist, and with that, arguably, the first bureau’s. The network, for its part, has never acknowledged any of them.

What remains is the SOC: one room, two monitors per analyst, no windows, and a policy stating that the lights must not be fully extinguished, ever. The policy is honored. The policy is the only policy that is honored.

§ 03

Required Forms

all fields mandatory
FORM 12-B

Request to Escalate

Required for all escalations. Approval of form 12-B is granted upon submission of form 12-B. Processing time: see form 12-B.

FORM 7

Declaration of an Incident

An incident exists only once declared. Undeclared incidents are classified as events, and events are nobody’s responsibility, which is why there are so many of them.

FORM 19-A

Request to Close a Ticket

Must be countersigned by the ticket’s creator. Tickets whose creators have departed are, by definition, open. Most tickets are, by definition, open.

FORM 33

Request for Daylight

Filed nightly by the analyst, as tradition requires. The request is always received and always applauded. There is no window budget. There has never been a window budget.

FORM 0

Request to Leave

Available at the front desk, which closes at the start of each shift. Alternative procedure: see form 12-B.

FORM 12-B

Request to Escalate (Duplicate)

There are two forms 12-B. Both are the correct one. Submitting to one voids the other, retrospectively. Legal advises submitting to both.
