ENTRY 0001 03:33 — duration: ongoing known behavior do not close

The Alert That Was Always There

Every night at 03:33 the same host sends a single ICMP echo to a domain that no longer resolves. NXDOMAIN. The ping goes nowhere and arrives. It has done this since before my assignment, and my predecessor’s, and his predecessor’s, who left a note in the runbook consisting of the words known behavior and a date.

I asked the day shift what is known. They said it is known. I asked where it is documented. They pointed at the runbook. The runbook says known behavior. The circle is complete and load-bearing.

I have started greeting the alert when it fires. Good evening. It does not answer, but then, neither does anyone here.

Recommendation: none available. Closing this alert requires the approval of its creator, who retired before the ticketing system existed.

ENTRY 0002 01:47 — duration: concluded? phishing no third floor

The Occupant

A phishing email arrived at 01:47 addressed to The Occupant, Third Floor. The building has two floors and a basement. The basement is where the printers live, and where I live, in the colloquial sense of the word, which is the only sense the basement permits.

The lure promised an invoice. There was no invoice. There was an attachment named invoice.pdf.exe, which is either the work of a novice or of someone who has learned, correctly, that no one reads extensions.

At 02:15, someone clicked. The click originated from the basement. I am in the basement. I did not click. I have reviewed the packet capture four times, and each time the source MAC is one I recognize.

The printers deny everything. Printers always deny everything.

ENTRY 0003 every 4 hours — since 2011 pending sla: breached

Ticket #4471, Pending

Ticket #4471 was opened in 2011 by an analyst whose badge number is mine, which is a clerical matter I am told not to pursue. The ticket’s summary reads: follow up on the below. There is no below. There was never a below.

The SLA requires a response within four hours. The system honors this faithfully: every four hours, it sends an apology. I have read 12,000 apologies. They are sincere. They are generated.

I attempted to close it once. The interface returned an error: CANNOT CLOSE: TICKET HAS DEPENDENCIES. I asked it to list the dependencies. It listed ticket #4471.

Tonight I replied to the apology. I wrote: understood. I will not tell you what it replied. There was nothing to tell. That is what I will not tell you.

ENTRY 0004 04:12 — duration: brief false positive do not open

False Positive

The DLP rule fired at 04:12 on a document consisting entirely of whitespace. Fourteen pages of it. The alert classified the content as potential data exfiltration, high confidence. I admire the confidence.

The file was named resignation_letter_FINAL_v7.docx. I closed the alert. I did not open the file. Some containment is procedural; some is mercy.

Later I considered that fourteen blank pages is, in this building, the most complete document ever produced, and that the DLP flagged it precisely for this reason. The machines know us. That is the problem with the machines, and also, lately, the only comfort.

There will be a v8. There is always a v8. I hope, for the author, it stays blank.

ENTRY 0005 03:00 — interval: 300s c2 suspected escalated: to whom

The Beacon

A host is beaconing at exactly 300-second intervals. No jitter. Five minutes, precisely, like a kettle, like a liturgy, like something that has never been afraid of being caught. Beacons with no jitter are either sophisticated or do not believe you exist. I have not decided which frightens me.

The C2 domain resolves to a decommissioned satellite ground station. I checked the registration: the owner of record is a company that was dissolved before it was founded. The whois record lists a phone number. I did not call it. Some pivots are lateral; some are down.

I attempted escalation. The tree lists me, and above me, the Committee, which meets once a year and has not yet been formed. I have therefore escalated to myself, and I have declined to take action pending further review, which is correct procedure, and which I understand completely, and which is the saddest sentence I have ever written in triplicate.

ENTRY 0006 04:12 — duration: 90 seconds benign logged anyway

Coffee, 04:12

Not everything is anomalous. The kettle boiled. The fluorescent tube in the corridor flickered twice and then resumed its single, sustained note. I made coffee. I logged it anyway: 04:12 — thermal event, kitchenette, expected process, no action required.

In this place even the uneventful feels uneventful on purpose, as if the night is withholding something and wants me to notice the withholding. I notice. I always notice. That is the job, and the job is noticing, and the noticing does not end, and the coffee is bad, and I will make another at 05:00, and I will log that too.

If you are reading this, and you work nights: the kettle is not anomalous. Log it anyway. It helps to write down one thing that is fine.
